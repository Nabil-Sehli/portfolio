Background

How I got here

I hold a European Bachelor's in IT and Networks, specialised in database development and SQL, with a state-certified Higher Technician Diploma in system administration and networking behind it. That combination is why deployment has never felt like somebody else's department: I set up the host, the reverse proxy and the backup schedule for my own projects, and I would rather understand the whole path from a schema change to a running container than hand half of it off.

I pick projects in domains that come with real rules, because that's where the interesting engineering is. A language-learning platform has to model CEFR levels and keep tenants isolated; a care-documentation app has to satisfy an inspector who will ask who wrote a record and whether anyone altered it since. Neither is solved by another CRUD screen.

What I care about most is the part that fails quietly: a deploy that half-succeeds, a schema tool that drops a column it thinks is redundant, an audit log that can be altered by the person it's meant to hold accountable. Those are the decisions I write down, and the ones I'd want to talk through in an interview.
